PREVENTIVE CARE AND LIFESTYLE GUIDELINES

GENERAL PREVENTIVE HEALTH
Preventive care focuses on identifying risk factors early before disease develops. Regular health screenings allow intervention before conditions become serious. Lifestyle modifications can reverse prediabetes, reduce cardiovascular risk, and prevent progression of many chronic conditions. The most impactful preventive lifestyle factors are regular physical activity, healthy diet, adequate sleep, stress management, not smoking, and limiting alcohol.

PHYSICAL ACTIVITY GUIDELINES
WHO recommends 150-300 minutes of moderate intensity aerobic activity per week for adults. Moderate intensity activities include brisk walking, cycling, swimming, and dancing. Alternatively 75-150 minutes of vigorous intensity activity such as running, fast cycling, or aerobic sports. Muscle strengthening activities involving major muscle groups are recommended 2 or more days per week. Reducing sedentary time is beneficial regardless of activity level. Even light physical activity is better than none. Exercise improves insulin sensitivity, reduces cardiovascular risk, helps manage weight, improves mood, and strengthens bones and muscles. Walking 30 minutes daily after meals is particularly effective for blood sugar control.

SLEEP AND RECOVERY
Adults need 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night. Sleep deprivation increases cortisol, disrupts glucose metabolism, raises blood pressure, and increases inflammation. Chronic poor sleep is associated with obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and impaired immune function. Sleep hygiene practices include consistent sleep and wake times, dark and cool bedroom, avoiding screens one hour before bed, and limiting caffeine after 2 PM.

STRESS MANAGEMENT
Chronic stress elevates cortisol which raises blood glucose, promotes fat storage, disrupts hormonal balance including thyroid function, and increases cardiovascular risk. Effective stress management approaches include meditation, yoga, deep breathing exercises, regular physical activity, adequate sleep, time in nature, and social connection. Mindfulness-based stress reduction has clinical evidence for reducing blood pressure, improving blood sugar control, and reducing inflammation.

MONITORING FREQUENCY GUIDELINES
Blood glucose should be monitored daily for people with diabetes and monthly for prediabetes. Lipid panels should be checked every 3-6 months when treating high cholesterol and annually for prevention. HbA1c should be checked every 3 months for diabetes management and every 6 months for prediabetes monitoring. Thyroid TSH should be rechecked every 6-12 months when on thyroid medication. Vitamin D levels should be rechecked after 3 months of supplementation. Kidney function tests should be repeated every 3-6 months if creatinine is elevated. Uric acid should be rechecked 6-8 weeks after dietary changes.

CARDIOVASCULAR RISK REDUCTION
Heart disease remains the leading cause of death globally. Major modifiable risk factors include high blood pressure, high LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, high triglycerides, diabetes, obesity, smoking, physical inactivity, and poor diet. Addressing multiple risk factors simultaneously provides greater protection than targeting individual factors. Mediterranean diet pattern, regular aerobic exercise, not smoking, and maintaining healthy weight are the most evidence-based interventions for cardiovascular risk reduction.

DIABETES PREVENTION PROGRAM
The Diabetes Prevention Program showed that lifestyle intervention reduced diabetes risk by 58% compared to 31% for metformin. The key components were 7% body weight reduction and 150 minutes per week of moderate physical activity. Even losing 5-10% of body weight improves insulin sensitivity and blood glucose significantly. Every kilogram of weight loss reduces diabetes risk by approximately 16%.

BONE HEALTH AND VITAMIN D
Adequate vitamin D and calcium intake throughout life builds peak bone mass and prevents osteoporosis. Calcium needs are 1000-1200 mg daily for adults. Dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens, almonds, and sardines with bones are good calcium sources. Weight bearing exercise including walking, jogging, and resistance training maintains bone density. Smoking and excess alcohol reduce bone density.

HYDRATION
Adequate hydration is essential for kidney function, metabolic processes, temperature regulation, and nutrient transport. General recommendation is 8-10 glasses of water daily. Needs increase with physical activity, heat, illness, and certain medical conditions. Urine color is a practical guide: pale yellow indicates adequate hydration. Water is the best choice. Avoid sugary beverages which raise blood glucose and triglycerides.

FOLLOW-UP CARE SCHEDULE
After receiving abnormal lab results, a follow-up appointment with a physician within 2-4 weeks is recommended for high priority findings such as significantly elevated blood glucose, very high cholesterol, elevated liver enzymes, or abnormal kidney function. Moderate abnormalities warrant follow-up within 4-8 weeks. Lifestyle changes should be sustained for at least 3 months before reassessing effectiveness. Patients should track symptoms, dietary changes, and physical activity between appointments.
