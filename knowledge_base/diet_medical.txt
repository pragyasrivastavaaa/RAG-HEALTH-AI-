CLINICAL NUTRITION AND DIET GUIDELINES FOR LAB ABNORMALITIES

DIET FOR ANEMIA (LOW HEMOGLOBIN)
Iron-rich foods are the foundation of anemia management. Heme iron from animal sources has higher bioavailability: red meat, liver, chicken, fish, and eggs. Non-heme iron from plant sources includes spinach, lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans, tofu, pumpkin seeds, quinoa, and fortified cereals. Vitamin C consumed with non-heme iron significantly enhances absorption. Excellent vitamin C sources include citrus fruits, bell peppers, strawberries, kiwi, and broccoli. Avoid tea, coffee, and calcium-rich foods within one hour of iron-rich meals as they inhibit absorption. Cooking in cast iron cookware can increase dietary iron content. Folate-rich foods support red blood cell production: dark leafy greens, legumes, avocado, and fortified grains.

DIET FOR DIABETES AND HIGH BLOOD SUGAR
The glycemic index approach helps manage blood sugar. Low GI foods include most vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts, and seeds. High GI foods to minimize include white rice, white bread, sugary beverages, candy, pastries, and processed snacks. Complex carbohydrates from oats, brown rice, quinoa, sweet potatoes, and whole wheat bread release glucose slowly. Fiber slows digestion and moderates blood sugar spikes. Aim for 25-35 grams of fiber daily. Bitter gourd (karela) contains polypeptide-P which mimics insulin and helps lower blood sugar. Fenugreek seeds contain soluble fiber that slows carbohydrate absorption. Cinnamon may improve insulin sensitivity. Chromium-rich foods like broccoli, nuts, and whole grains support insulin function. Protein at each meal helps stabilize blood sugar. Portion control and meal timing every 3-4 hours prevents large glucose fluctuations.

DIET FOR HIGH CHOLESTEROL
Soluble fiber reduces LDL cholesterol by binding cholesterol in the digestive tract. Best sources include oats, barley, psyllium husk, apples, oranges, beans, and Brussels sprouts. Aim for 10-25 grams of soluble fiber daily to reduce LDL by 3-5%. Plant sterols and stanols found in nuts, seeds, vegetable oils, and fortified foods block cholesterol absorption. Omega-3 fatty acids from fatty fish reduce triglycerides and inflammation. Best sources include salmon, mackerel, sardines, herring, and anchovies. Plant omega-3 sources include flaxseeds, chia seeds, walnuts, and hemp seeds. Unsaturated fats from olive oil, avocado, nuts, and seeds support healthy cholesterol ratios. Saturated fats from butter, coconut oil, fatty red meat, and full-fat dairy raise LDL. Trans fats from partially hydrogenated oils found in commercial fried foods and baked goods are the most harmful for cholesterol and should be eliminated entirely. Mediterranean diet pattern consistently reduces cardiovascular risk.

DIET FOR HIGH TRIGLYCERIDES
Sugar and refined carbohydrates are the primary dietary drivers of high triglycerides. Eliminate sugary drinks, fruit juices, candy, white bread, and white rice. Alcohol significantly raises triglycerides and should be avoided. Omega-3 fatty acids are clinically proven to lower triglycerides by 15-30%. Fatty fish twice weekly or fish oil supplementation is recommended. Complex carbohydrates from vegetables, legumes, and whole grains replace simple carbs. Portion control of all carbohydrates is important even for healthy sources. Berries, apples, and pears are lower fructose fruit choices. Avoid high fructose fruits like mangoes, grapes, and bananas in excess.

DIET FOR THYROID SUPPORT
Iodine is essential for thyroid hormone production. Food sources include iodized salt, seafood, dairy, and eggs. Selenium supports thyroid hormone conversion and protects the gland. Best sources are Brazil nuts (1-2 daily), sunflower seeds, fish, eggs, and mushrooms. Zinc supports thyroid function. Sources include pumpkin seeds, cashews, chickpeas, beef, and oysters. For hypothyroidism, cooking cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, kale) neutralizes goitrogens that can interfere with thyroid function. Gluten sensitivity may affect thyroid autoimmune conditions. Anti-inflammatory foods including turmeric, ginger, berries, and fatty fish support thyroid health.

DIET FOR VITAMIN D DEFICIENCY
Dietary sources of vitamin D include fatty fish (salmon, tuna, mackerel), fish liver oils, egg yolks, beef liver, cheese, and fortified foods such as milk, orange juice, and cereals. However diet alone rarely corrects deficiency. Morning sunlight exposure for 15-20 minutes on arms and legs is the most effective way to produce vitamin D. Darker skin requires longer sun exposure. Vitamin D3 supplementation is more effective than D2 at raising blood levels. Magnesium is required for vitamin D metabolism. Magnesium-rich foods include dark leafy greens, nuts, seeds, whole grains, and legumes.

DIET FOR VITAMIN B12 DEFICIENCY
Animal products are the only natural dietary sources of B12. These include meat, poultry, fish, shellfish, eggs, and dairy products. For vegetarians and vegans, fortified foods are essential: plant-based milks, nutritional yeast, fortified breakfast cereals, and fortified soy products. Fermented foods like tempeh and miso contain small amounts. B12 absorption requires intrinsic factor produced in the stomach. Conditions that affect stomach acid production reduce B12 absorption. Elderly individuals absorb B12 less efficiently and may need supplementation. Sublingual B12 supplements bypass stomach absorption issues.

DIET FOR KIDNEY HEALTH (HIGH CREATININE)
Reduce protein intake moderately as excess protein produces urea that stresses kidneys. Choose plant proteins over animal proteins when possible. Limit sodium to under 2000 mg daily. Avoid processed and canned foods high in sodium. Stay well hydrated with 8-10 glasses of water daily. Kidney-friendly foods include cauliflower, cabbage, garlic, onions, apples, cranberries, and olive oil. Limit potassium-rich foods if kidney disease is confirmed: bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, and dairy. Avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) which reduce kidney blood flow.

DIET FOR HIGH URIC ACID AND GOUT
Foods high in purines break down into uric acid. High purine foods to avoid include organ meats, red meat, shellfish, anchovies, sardines, and alcohol especially beer. Moderate purine foods to limit include chicken, beef, and pork. Cherries, both sweet and tart, significantly reduce uric acid levels and gout attacks. Consume 10-12 cherries daily or drink tart cherry juice. Low-fat dairy products actively reduce uric acid levels. Drink 8-10 glasses of water daily to excrete uric acid through urine. High fructose corn syrup increases uric acid production. Vitamin C supplementation lowers uric acid levels. Coffee consumption is associated with lower uric acid in studies.

DIET FOR LIVER HEALTH (HIGH LIVER ENZYMES)
Coffee and green tea contain antioxidants that protect liver cells. Two or more cups of coffee daily is associated with lower liver enzyme levels. Cruciferous vegetables including broccoli, Brussels sprouts, and cabbage contain compounds that enhance liver detoxification. Garlic activates liver enzymes that flush out toxins. Beets and beet juice contain betaine which protects liver cells. Walnuts contain arginine that helps the liver detoxify ammonia. Grapefruit contains antioxidants that protect the liver. Olive oil reduces liver fat accumulation. Avoid alcohol completely. Avoid high fructose foods which contribute to fatty liver. Maintain healthy weight as obesity is a leading cause of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease.
